RESEARCH · CYBERSECURITY

Progressive Cyber Defense

Exploring how university networks can strengthen cyber defense incrementally when visibility, asset information, and resources are limited.

CybersecurityNetwork Monitoring Risk AnalysisResilience

01. Research Overview

University networks support a diverse and changing set of users, devices, and services. This research considers a practical way to improve defensive visibility and response over time rather than assuming that every asset and event can be observed at once. The motivation is to make the best use of available information and resources while building toward more complete coverage.

02. Problem Definition

The research focuses on three related constraints: partial visibility into network activity, limited time and resources for defense, and incomplete or changing information about connected assets. These constraints can make it difficult to decide what should be monitored or addressed first.

03. Research Objectives

The main objective is to investigate a progressive, risk-informed approach to cyber defense that can improve as evidence and capacity grow. Specific objectives are to organize known assets and visibility gaps, describe a repeatable prioritization model, and identify how monitoring evidence can inform the next defensive improvement.

04. Literature Review

A literature review for this work should compare asset management, continuous monitoring, risk-based prioritization, incident response, and cybersecurity maturity approaches. It should identify how those approaches handle incomplete inventories and resource constraints. This page does not claim a completed comparison or a specific research gap until the sources and review are documented.

05. Proposed Framework

The proposed concept is a feedback loop: establish what is known, record gaps and available evidence, prioritize the most meaningful risks within current capacity, apply an approved defensive improvement, and review whether visibility or risk has changed. Each cycle should leave a clear record of decisions and unresolved uncertainty.

06. System Architecture

At a conceptual level, the framework connects an asset and service inventory, authorized monitoring sources, an evidence and prioritization layer, and a response or improvement process. Findings and coverage gaps feed back into the inventory and the next review cycle. The design is intended to be adaptable; it does not assume access to every system or data source.

07. Security Assessment

Assessment areas include asset inventory coverage, the availability of security updates and configuration records, documented vulnerabilities, and whether defensive controls have evidence of operation. Any validation must be explicitly authorized, limited to a written scope, and carried out in an appropriate controlled environment. This research page does not report findings against a live network.

08. Monitoring & Evidence Collection

Potential evidence sources include approved network monitoring, traffic summaries, system and security logs, and records of defensive changes. Evidence should be collected with appropriate authorization and privacy safeguards, then documented with its source, time period, and known limitations so that decisions remain traceable.

09. Decision-Making Approach

Prioritization can consider potential impact, confidence in the evidence, exposure, service importance, and the effort required to respond. When information is incomplete, uncertainty should be recorded rather than hidden. The intent is to direct limited resources toward justified improvements and reassess priorities as new evidence becomes available.

10. Implementation & Demonstration

A demonstration, if conducted, should use an isolated lab or other explicitly authorized environment with a documented scope. Tools identified for possible supervised use include Kali Linux, Nmap, Greenbone/OpenVAS, and Wireshark. Listing these tools does not indicate that a scan or assessment has been performed; no operational testing procedure is provided here.

11. Experimental Results

No verified discovery counts, vulnerability findings, monitoring observations, or comparative measurements have been supplied for this page. Results should be added only after an authorized, repeatable evaluation, with the scope, method, and limitations clearly recorded.

12. Limitations

Incomplete asset information and limited monitoring can leave blind spots. Access restrictions, privacy requirements, staff capacity, and unavailable evidence can also constrain the assessment. Priorities derived from partial evidence should therefore be treated as provisional and reviewed as conditions change.

13. Future Improvements

Possible future work includes integrating authorized evidence sources, improving risk prioritization with better asset context, measuring defensive coverage over time, and exploring how findings could be shared with a security operations centre (SOC) where one is available.
