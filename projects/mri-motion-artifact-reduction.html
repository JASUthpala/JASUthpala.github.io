MRI Motion Artifact Reduction

Exploring GAN-based approaches to reduce rigid motion artifacts in abdominal MRI while preserving meaningful structural details.

PythonGANsTensorFlow KerasMedical Imaging

Research overview

This research focuses on reducing rigid motion artifacts in abdominal Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Breathing and patient movement can introduce ghosting, blurring, and structural distortions that affect medical image interpretation. The study investigates reconstructing motion-free images from corrupted inputs, with particular attention to generator loss combinations and weight initialization strategies.

Research objectives

The study aims to develop a GAN-based framework for abdominal MRI motion-artifact reduction, simulate motion corruption through k-space manipulation, and compare the effect of generator loss combinations and weight initialization on reconstruction quality. Evaluation uses quantitative image-quality metrics and visual comparisons.

Methodology

The experiments use 5,000 abdominal MRI images from Duke Liver Dataset v2. DICOM images were preprocessed, normalized, resized to 512 × 512 pixels, and split into 80% training and 20% testing.

  • Motion simulation: Applied spatial shifts to clean images and replaced selected k-space frequency bands to create paired motion-corrupted and clean samples.
  • GAN model: Used a residual U-Net encoder-decoder generator and a discriminator to distinguish restored images from clean images.
  • Loss comparisons: Compared GAN + L1, GAN + L1 + perceptual, GAN + L1 + SSIM, and GAN + L1 + perceptual + SSIM.
  • Evaluation: Compared MSE, PSNR, and SSIM, training loss curves, and reconstructed MRI images.

GAN architecture

The generator estimates a residual artifact map from a motion-corrupted image and subtracts it to produce a restored image. The discriminator compares generated outputs with clean MRI images during adversarial training.

Results

The reported quantitative results show different trade-offs between pixel-level error, signal fidelity, and structural similarity across the generator loss configurations.

Model configuration MSE PSNR (dB) SSIM

  • GAN + L1 achieved the lowest reported MSE and highest PSNR.
  • GAN + L1 + Perceptual achieved the highest reported SSIM.
  • Adding SSIM loss or combining more loss terms did not consistently improve all reported metrics.

Limitations and future work

The evaluation uses synthetically generated motion artifacts, focuses on translational motion, and does not include clinical validation by radiologists. Future work could model respiratory and non-rigid motion, evaluate real motion-corrupted clinical MRI, study loss weighting, and include expert review.
